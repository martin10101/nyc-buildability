## 1. Verdict

**PASS with required corrections.**

The question put to this gate was whether 37 tests for 8 acceptance scenarios is *enough* or merely
*green*. My answer, from 49 mutations run against a scratch copy: **enough for every headline claim,
thin on breadth.** Every one of AS-1..AS-8 is protected by at least one demonstrably load-bearing
test, and the correctness heart of the task — transport-never-interpret — is the **best-covered part
of the pack**: every reformat, round, coercion, humanised timestamp and dropped record I could inject
into a citation, a cap or a provenance record went RED. The two M5-T012 findings the packet carried
forward are genuinely closed, including the subtle one: dropping the
`ensure_ascii=False … .encode("utf-8")` half of the serialisation check **does** fail a test, and an
unpaired-surrogate document **is** exercised.

But **17 of 49 mutations survived**, and they cluster into five material gaps. The most valuable:
**the entire `source_coverage` block — 8 of its 10 fields — can be silently emptied, type-coerced or
replaced and all 37 tests stay green** (H-1). For an endpoint whose whole premise is "every material
value is transported verbatim", roughly 60% of the emitted document's fields carry no transport
assertion at all. The product is correct today (I verified transport behaviour directly for every
gap), so these are test-coverage holes, not defects — which is why this is a PASS and not a FAIL.

Per `.claude/rules/project-control.md` gate-verdict semantics this is recorded as **PASS**, with
corrections **H-1..H-5 BLOCKING for the next gate and for acceptance**. Every one is one assertion or
one unit test; H-5 and H-6 cost no fixtures and no runtime.

## 4. Findings by severity

### BLOCKING for the next gate and for acceptance

**H-1 — MEDIUM/HIGH — whole emitted sub-blocks have no verbatim-transport assertion; the
`source_coverage` block can be silently emptied and all 37 tests stay green.**
Seven mutations survive, all in the same class: `source_coverage.reasons` joined into a string (A14),
`data_completeness` → `{}` (B1), `family_coverage` → `[]` (B2), `rule_lifecycle_statuses` → `[]`
(B3), `evaluated_input.bbl` → `"9999999999"` (D2), `evaluated_input.profile_contract_version` →
`"0.0.0"` (D3), and the `rule_citations` group dropping `rule_version` + `family` (D7).
Product locations: `services/api/app/api/v1/evidence.py:328-343` (source_coverage), `:346-351`
(evaluated_input), `:294-312` (rule_citations group).
Cause: the pack's two deep-equality assertions
(`test_as8_direct_assembly_is_pure_transport`, `services/api/tests/api/test_evidence_api.py:860-878`)
cover only `profile_provenance`, `input_provenance`, `coverage_status` and `not_verified_disclaimer`;
`test_as1_evidence_document_carries_the_whole_trail_verbatim` (`:281-334`) compares **projections**
(`_prov_identity` = 3 of 20 provenance fields, `_cit_identity` = 3 of 5 citation fields) rather than
whole records. Of the emitted document's fields, 8 of 10 in `source_coverage`, 2 of 4 in
`evaluated_input` and 2 of 7 per `rule_citations` group are unasserted.
This matters more here than it would elsewhere: AS-1/AS-2 make "every material value transported
verbatim" the defining promise of the endpoint, and `source_coverage` is precisely the
machine-readable honesty posture (`needs_review`, `fail_safe`, `data_completeness`, `family_coverage`)
the evidence view exists to make legible.
*Minimal fix (not applied):* extend the existing direct-assembly test with one deep-equality
assertion per block — `document["source_coverage"][k] == rule_eval[k]` for all ten keys,
`document["evaluated_input"][k] == rule_eval["evaluated_input"][k]` for all four, and the group
scalars against their trace. It is the pattern already in that test; no new fixture, no extra runtime.

**H-2 — MEDIUM — AS-6's fetch-stage exception guard is untested.**
`services/api/app/api/v1/evidence.py:438-442` is a fourth generic guard (the packet's AS-6 names
rebuild, assembly and serialisation); removing it leaves all 37 tests green (C1). No test drives a
non-`PlutoConnectorError` raise out of the injected fetcher — I confirmed the pack never mentions
`stage=fetch`, and its only fetcher-raiser is the landmine, used on paths that return before the
fetch. The product is correct: I drove a `RuntimeError` through the real handler and got
`500 / application/json / state=internal_error / X-Correlation-ID present / no "secret" leak`. So a
regression here would be invisible to the pack while being exactly the M5-T012 G1/G5 HIGH-1 defect
class the packet set out to pre-empt.
*Minimal fix:* one test overriding `get_pluto_fetcher` with a `RuntimeError`-raising provider,
asserting the typed `(500, "internal_error")` pair, JSON content-type, correlation id and no leak —
a copy of `test_as6_rebuild_stage_raise_is_typed_internal_error_500` (`:635-654`) with a different
seam.

**H-3 — MEDIUM — AS-7's never-Verified check is an enumeration, so a NEW server-authored claim key is
invisible.**
`_server_authored_statuses` (`services/api/tests/api/test_evidence_api.py:266-274`) lists four known
paths. Adding `"verified": True` + `"verification": "verified"` to the document top level (C6) or
`"verified": True` inside every `rule_citations` group (D1) both ship green.
Credit where due: the *scoping* this gate recommended after M5-T012 **is** implemented as asked — the
invariant is explicitly limited to server-authored fields, comparison is case-insensitive
(`str(status).strip().lower()`, `:733`), and the limit is documented on the document itself and
asserted (`verification_scope_note`, `:736-737`, product text at `evidence.py:144-153`). That scope
note is a genuinely good piece of work: it states why a blanket token scan is falsifiable instead of
pretending otherwise. The weakness is only that the check enumerates paths instead of walking.
*Minimal fix:* walk the document for verification-claim **keys** (`verified`, `is_verified`,
`verification`, `verification_status`) at every depth, requiring each to deny
(`value in (False, None)`), while excluding the transported free-prose paths (citation
`quote`/`provenance`) the scope note already carves out — the M5-T012 C3 shape, scoped as AS-7
requires.

**H-4 — MEDIUM — AS-3's "no query parameter" half is untested.**
AS-3 says the endpoint accepts "no request body, no query parameter". The pack proves the body half
well (`test_as3_request_body_cannot_influence_the_response`, `:405-428`, compares a stable projection
and asserts the injected value appears nowhere) but nothing tests query parameters: adding
`terse: bool = False` to the handler and stripping `profile_provenance` when it is set leaves all 37
tests green (B5). `include_in_schema=False` means OpenAPI cannot catch it either.
*Minimal fix:* extend the existing body test to also issue the request with arbitrary query
parameters (`?terse=1&cap=999&format=slim`) and assert the same stable projection, and/or assert the
handler signature exposes only `bbl` plus the two `Depends` parameters.

**H-5 — MEDIUM — 3 of 4 completeness labels and 2 of 4 gap kinds are unreachable with the available
fixtures, and the two pure classifier functions have no unit tests.**
Swapping `_COMPLETENESS_COMPLETE` ↔ `_COMPLETENESS_THIN` survives (B13). I probed why: **both**
available substrates yield `evidence_completeness == "professional_review_required"` (confident: 1
group / 1 citation; split-lot: 0 groups), because `needs_review` is true in both and
`_completeness_marker` (`evidence.py:217-231`) tests professional-review before thin. So `complete`,
`thin` and `conflicting` are never produced through the route, and the `data_conflict` /
`not_applicable` gap kinds and the `profile_provenance`-absent gap are never produced either. AS-5
names "thin, conflicting, unsupported or professional-review-required" — only the last is exercised.
*Minimal fix:* unit-test `_completeness_marker` and `_gap_markers` directly with synthetic
`rule_evaluation` dicts covering each branch. They are pure functions over plain dicts: no fixture,
no HTTP request, no measurable runtime.

### Non-blocking findings

**H-6 — LOW/MEDIUM — "every rule citation" is only ever exercised at n=1 (and the thin case at n=0).**
A5 (reverse citation order) and B4 (emit only the first evaluation group) survive *necessarily*: no
available fixture/substrate produces more than one rule group or more than one citation per rule, so
both mutations are no-ops. Aggregation and ordering across multiple rules/citations — the "every" in
AS-1(b) — is structurally untestable as the pack stands. *Fix:* pass a synthetic multi-rule,
multi-citation `rule_evaluation` dict straight to `assemble_evidence_document` (the pack already
calls it directly at `:860-878`) and assert count and order.

**H-7 — LOW — type fidelity: a float → int cap coercion is invisible.** A6 survives because `==` is
type-insensitive in Python (`15000 == 15000.0`), so `round()`-ing the cap passes
`assert evidence_cap == trace_cap == TRACE_CAP` (`:352-355`). For a document whose premise is
byte-equal transport of legal values, silently changing `15000.0` to `15000` is a real if cosmetic
fidelity loss. *Fix:* compare the serialised form (or `type()`) for at least the cap.

**H-8 — LOW — the document-version assertion is self-referential.** Bumping
`EVIDENCE_CONTRACT_VERSION` to `9.9.9` keeps the pack green (D4) because the test imports the
constant it is checking; only a hardcoded wrong literal is caught (D5). Same pattern as M5-T012's C5.
*Fix:* pin the literal `"1.0.0"` in one assertion.

**H-9 — LOW/INFO — `test_as2_module_does_no_arithmetic_or_reevaluation` (`:360-375`) is a weak
structural proxy that gives false comfort.** It greps the module for `build_scenario` and
`import math` and counts `evaluate_property(` — none of which detects `round()`, `*`, or `str()`
coercion. A6 survived all 37 tests including this one, and A8 (real arithmetic on the cap) was caught
by the *value* assertion in the sibling test, not by this one. Keep it as a cheap guard against
re-evaluation, but the byte-equality assertions are what actually hold the line; AS-2's "the module
contains no arithmetic on a legal value" is not provable by grep.

**H-10 — INFO — the `allow_nan=False` half of `_assert_json_safe` is redundant and untested.**
Removing `evidence.py:372` leaves 37 green (C5): the second call carries `allow_nan=False` too, so the
parametrised NaN/Infinity cases are caught by the `ensure_ascii=False` form alone. Not a defect — the
packet requires both forms and both are present, and keeping the explicit pair documents the
M5-T012 lesson. Recorded only so nobody mistakes its survival for a gap.
