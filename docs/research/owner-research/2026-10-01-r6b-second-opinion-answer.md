## First answer (short)
Yes, general R6 rules also apply to R6B unless the zoning text expressly provides a different rule or exclusion (§11-25).
- "R6" with no separate R6B provision or exclusion → applies to R6B.
- "R6 districts without a letter suffix" → does not apply to R6B.
- Separate R6 and R6B provisions or table rows → use the R6B provision.

Example: §23-432 lists "R6" but has a separate R6B row. Example: §23-434 applies only to districts without a letter suffix, so its eligible-site height modifications do not automatically apply to R6B.

## Follow-up answer (states it checked the current official text; §§23-362, 23-363, 23-344, 23-52 last amended 12/5/2024)

### 1a. Lot coverage
§23-362 sets no separate R6B limit and reaches R6B through §11-25. Under (a):
- interior or through lots: 80 percent;
- corner lots: 100 percent.

§23-363 gives possible increases, not a different R6B limit: eligible shallow lots (with their own 90% overall cap), qualifying interior/through portions near street intersections, and qualifying portions along a block's short dimension. The last two can reach 100%.

The 65%/50% limits in §23-362(b) are for §23-434 eligible-site projects. §23-434 applies only to districts without a letter suffix, so not ordinarily to R6B. Coverage remains subject to yards and other requirements.

### 1b. Rear yard near the corner
§23-344(a) applies in R6B: "no rear yard shall be required" within 100 feet of the intersection point of two street lines meeting at 135 degrees or less. The section includes R6 with no R6B exclusion.

### 1c. Dwelling units
§23-52(b): "For all other types of multiple dwelling residences, the applicable dwelling unit factor shall be 680." So max units = max residential floor area ÷ 680, and a remainder of 0.75 or more rounds up.

Paragraph (a) exempts:
- special density areas, which §12-10 defines as the Manhattan Core and the Special Downtown Brooklyn District;
- qualifying senior housing;
- specified conversions.

So the location and project type matter as well as the R6B district.

### 2. C2-2 overlay on R6B
- **Residential-only zoning lot.** §34-111 includes C2-2: "the bulk regulations for the Residence District within which such Commercial Districts are mapped apply". Its listed substitutions are for lower-density districts, not R6B.
- **Mixed buildings.** §35-22: the underlying district's rules "apply to residential portions of buildings". §§35-01, 35-10 and 35-21 set the framework.

Where each rule comes from:
- **Height and setback.**
  - Residential-only: §34-111, modified by §34-24(b)(1), which imports §35-63.
  - Mixed building: §§35-22, 35-24(a)(1) and 35-63.
- **Residential coverage.**
  - Residential-only: §§23-362–363 via §34-111.
  - Mixed building: via §§35-21–22.
- **Residential rear yards.**
  - Residential-only: §§23-342–344 via §34-111.
  - Mixed building: the same rules, plus §§35-50 and 35-53, with an option under §35-24(b).

What changes and what doesn't:
- **Heights stay R6B.** §35-632(a) uses "the table in Section 23-432 for the applicable Residence District". The R6B row gives the minimum base height, maximum base height and maximum building height, and §23-433 gives the setbacks.
- **Street-wall placement CHANGES, even for residential-only buildings.** §34-24(b)(1) imports §35-63, and §35-631(b) says: "At least 70 percent of the aggregate width of street walls shall be located within eight feet of the street line". There are qualifications for prevailing street walls, large lots and articulation. Plain R6B instead starts from the adjacent-building lineup in §23-431(a).
- **Mixed-building rear yard.** §35-53: a required residential rear yard is located "at the floor level of the lowest story used for dwelling units or rooming units", where windows face it. §35-50 gives the general rule. This does not erase an otherwise required yard.
- **The commercial portion.** It is governed by Article III Chapter 3 under §35-10, including §33-26 and the corner exception in §33-301. §35-24(b)(1) allows applying the residential rear-yard rules to the whole building. The 80% residential coverage is not automatically a cap on a commercial ground-floor footprint.

### 3. A roughly 100 × 100 ft corner lot
- **The corner portion.** §12-10 (amended 5/20/1965) defines it by "lines parallel to and 100 feet from each intersecting street line". Remaining portions get interior/through treatment.
- **An exact square is all corner portion.** An exactly rectangular 100 × 100 lot at a right-angle intersection, with both frontages meeting there, lies entirely in the corner portion. The ~141 ft diagonal does not create an interior portion. §23-342 imposes rear yards on interior lots.
- **§23-344(c).** It covers interior/through portions of corner lots: the side-lot-line portion "beyond 100 feet of the street line that it intersects" "shall be considered a rear lot line". The result depends on what it adjoins:
  - adjoining a rear lot line → (c)(1): provide the §23-342 rear yard;
  - adjoining a side lot line → (c)(3): no rear yard.
- **A required §23-342 yard is not always 30 ft.** For example, an attached or semi-detached building on a standard lot at least 40 ft wide generally needs 20 ft at or below 75 ft.
- **Unresolved facts:** the exact surveyed dimensions, the street angle, and the adjoining lots' lot-line classifications.
