EVIDENCE - r6b-side-yard (ZR 23-335 / 34-232) - a labelled unreviewed DRAFT (ADR-007)

LAW (captured, pinned by content digest):
- ZR 23-335 (zr-23-335): "(a) Detached buildings ... for zoning lots containing only
  single-family or two-family detached residences, two side yards, each with a minimum width of
  five feet, shall be provided. (b) All other buildings ... for zoning lots containing all other
  types of residences, no side yards shall be required. However, if any open area extending along
  a side lot line is provided at any level, it shall have a minimum width of five feet, measured
  perpendicular to the side lot line."
- ZR 34-232 (zr-34-232): "In the districts indicated, no side yard shall be required for any
  residential building. However, if any open area extending along a side lot line is provided at
  any level, it shall have a minimum width of five feet ..."
- ZR 11-25 (zr-11-25): R6B inherits ZR 23 through the suffix provision.

READING (record NB-06, and session-2026-10-15a/return-research-mixed-side-yard.md for the mixed
building; both independent):
- For a residence other than a single- or two-family detached one (a multiple-dwelling or a mixed
  building), no side yard is required under ZR 23-335(b). Under the C2-2 overlay, ZR 34-232
  removes the requirement for any residential building. A mixed building reaches the same result
  through ZR 35-01 -> 35-21 -> 35-22 -> 23-335(b). (ZR 34-232 by its words reaches a "residential
  building"; ZR 35-24 is not captured and is not cited.)
- A single- or two-family detached residence needs two five-foot side yards under ZR 23-335(a)
  without the overlay; even that is removed by ZR 34-232 in the overlay.
- A side yard is not prohibited; it is optional with a five-foot open-area minimum if provided.
- A building type that is not stated is never taken as residential-only (the detached case stays
  a named condition, never a silent default).

This is the program's own draft reading of the law. It has not been reviewed by a qualified
architect or zoning examiner and is not legal advice. Not a determination by the Department of
Buildings.
